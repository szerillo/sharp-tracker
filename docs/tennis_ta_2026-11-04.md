# Tennis Abstract: sharpening the tennis models (2026-11-04)

Source: Jeff Sackmann's match database (ATP and WTA tour, Challenger, qualifying, ITF, futures) from the Aneeshers mirror of the
archive (the original GitHub repos no longer answer); 621,334 matches with a completed first set, Dec 2014 to June 2026, pulled
weekly by .github/workflows/tennis_data.yml into data/tennis/ta. Script: sharp/tennis_ta.py (research) and `--map` (the live model
and player profiles). Train = 2015-2022, holdout = 2023-2026 wherever a fit predicts. No prices in this data: these are structure
and skill results, not market edges. The market test of anything here runs on our own tennis price log (Pinnacle closes since 10/29).

## 1. Elo

Chronological Elo per tour, overall and per surface (K = 250 / (n + 5)^0.4, lower-level matches at 0.6x / 0.4x). Holdout log loss:
overall 0.601, 50/50 surface blend 0.603, surface-only 0.613. Elo is under-confident on favourites with 20+ matches (a 72% Elo
favourite wins 80%), so every mapping below uses the calibrated (observed) match rate as its x, never the raw Elo number.

## 2. First set vs match (the live rule's model)

Favourite's set-1 win rate against the calibrated match probability, best-of-3, all levels pooled (n per bin 10k to 120k):

| Match probability | Set-1 observed | Independent-sets model | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.59 | 0.573 | 0.561 | +1.2 |
| 0.66 | 0.629 | 0.606 | +2.3 |
| 0.72 | 0.685 | 0.653 | +3.2 |
| 0.79 | 0.738 | 0.705 | +3.3 |
| 0.84 | 0.787 | 0.748 | +3.9 |
| 0.88 | 0.829 | 0.797 | +3.2 |
| 0.91 | 0.860 | 0.835 | +2.5 |

Read: the favourite wins set 1 more often than independent sets imply, by 1 point near a coin flip and 3 to 4 points for 70-90%
favourites. The live rule's flat +3.3 was right for big favourites and 2 points too generous for 55-65% favourites. Level offsets
against the pooled curve are under 1 point (ATP tour -0.8, Challenger -0.4, ITF 0, WTA tour -0.4, WTA ITF +0.4); train and
holdout agree within 2 points in every group. Comeback structure (bo3): P(win match | won set 1) runs 0.83 (coin flip) to 0.98
(90% favourite); P(win match | lost set 1) 0.21 to 0.51. Best-of-5 (men's slams, n 6k): the gap is smaller (+1 to +2 to 75%),
larger above 85%.

## 3. Serve profile (matchup information that moves the fair set-1 price)

Trailing hold rate per player (last 15 tour-level matches with serve stats). Residual of the favourite's set-1 rate against the
pooled curve at the same match probability:

| Matchup | n | Set-1 observed | Curve | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dog holds 3%+ more than the favourite | 57,744 | 0.554 | 0.595 | -4.1 |
| Similar hold rates | 56,603 | 0.597 | 0.610 | -1.3 |
| Favourite holds 3%+ more | 118,217 | 0.655 | 0.642 | +1.3 |

Read: when the underdog is the bigger server, set 1 is much closer to a coin flip than the match price says (one break decides it,
and the dog's serve is harder to break). This is the one matchup input with a clear effect, and it is in the live model: -4.1 /
-1.3 / +1.3 points on the fair set-1 price when both players' hold rates are known (tour-level players; Challenger and ITF players
mostly have no stats).

## 4. Surface

Surface changes the set-1 structure not at all once the match price is known: residual vs the curve is within 0.3 points on hard,
clay and grass for both tours (grass has more tiebreaks, 22% of ATP first sets vs 13% on clay, but that is already in the match
price). Surface fit as a betting angle: when the surface-Elo favourite and the overall-Elo favourite disagree (22k holdout
matches), the surface favourite wins 46.6% (47% on hard and clay, 40% on grass; 46% even when the surface gap is 10%+). The
overall-stronger player is the better guide; surface specialisation is overrated. First-set games average 9.5 (ATP clay) to 10.1
(ATP grass), 9.1 to 9.5 for the WTA, for set-1 totals later.

## 5. Form, rest, rank

- Form (last-10 win rate) on top of Elo, holdout: favourite hotter by 30%+ wins 75.5% vs 68.5% Elo (+7.0; +3.1 against the
  similar-form baseline); dog hotter: 58.4% vs 59.6% (-1.2; -5.1 against baseline). Real information beyond Elo; whether it is beyond
  the market is the live test.
- Rest: not usable from this data (tourney_date is the tournament's start date, not the match day).
- Rank points vs Elo: when they pick different favourites, the Elo favourite wins 56.3% (both tours, every level). Elo is the
  better strength number; the market already knows this.

## 6. Live changes

- sharp/tennis_watch.py: fair set-1 = pooled curve at the Pinnacle no-vig match price + level offset + serve adjustment, capped at
  the match price minus 1 point; the old independent-sets + 3.3 number is kept on each row as `fair_old`. Player Elo, surface Elo
  and hold rates (data/state/tennis_profiles.json, through June 2026) show in the row details.
- Re-scored against the 32 paper picks to date (19-10 graded, CLV -1.83 points vs Pinnacle's set-1 close): 8 would still flag
  (6-2), 24 would not (13-8). The old rule's negative CLV is consistent with the model having been too generous on near-coin-flip
  favourites and on matches where the dog is the bigger server; the new model flags fewer, better-priced matches.

## 8. Round 2: rust, layoffs, surface switches, matchups, beyond set 1 (sharp/tennis_ta2.py, data/research/tennis_ta2.json)

Every cut is the favourite's residual against the calibrated Elo price (match) and against the pooled set-1 curve (set 1), in
points. A residual says the price (Elo) misses something; whether the market also misses it is the live test. Tour + Challenger
only where stated: the ITF files mix qualifying into the event, which makes the "first match" flags there meaningless (the raw
ITF numbers in the JSON are artifacts, not effects).

| Cut (tour + Challenger, best-of-3) | n | Match resid | Set-1 resid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Favourite's first match of the event, opponent has already played (holdout) | 7,145 | -1.6 | -2.2 |
| Dog's first match, favourite has already played (holdout) | 2,121 | +3.4 | +0.8 |
| Favourite off 28+ days since its last event (both in their first match) | 4,667 | -8.8 | -7.1 |
| Dog off 28+ days (both in their first match) | 6,810 | +6.9 | +5.9 |
| Favourite's last match was on another surface, dog's was not (both first match) | 5,917 | -2.7 (holdout -2.2) | -2.8 (holdout -3.7) |
| Dog switched surface, favourite did not | 7,840 | +4.1 (holdout +4.8) | +3.2 (holdout +4.4) |
| Dog leads the head-to-head (holdout) | 6,867 | -0.9 | -1.6 |
| Semi-finals, tour level | 2,541 | -2.9 | -1.4 |
| Dog is a lucky loser (all levels) | 6,755 | +5.5 | +4.5 |

Read:
- Rust from a bye is real but small: 1.5 to 2 points against the favourite who has not played yet, in the match and in set 1.
- A layoff is the big one: a favourite coming off 28+ days away wins 9 points less than its rating says and drops set 1 7 points
  more often. Some of that is injury the market knows about and Elo does not; how much is the question for the live test.
- A surface switch (first match on a new surface) costs about 3 points, and it shows up in set 1 as much as in the match: the
  "rusty on this surface" first set is real. Holds in the holdout.
- Head-to-head adds about a point beyond Elo at tour level; the market uses it more than that. Lefties: nothing. Age: Elo lag.
  Wild-card favourites underperform by 5, which is Elo not knowing a wild card's current level, not a bet.

## 9. The market test: Tennis Abstract features against the Pinnacle close (sharp/tennis_edge.py, data/research/tennis_edge.json)

Prices: tennis-data.co.uk's results-with-odds tables, as bundled in the deuce R package (the site itself refuses GitHub's runner).
That bundle is the four Grand Slam main draws a year per tour, 2010 to January 2023; joined to Tennis Abstract by names, event
window and set-1 score: 6,754 matches 2015-2023 (3,460 ATP best-of-5, 3,294 WTA), Pinnacle closing match price on every one. Match
market only (no set or games odds). "Edge" is the win rate minus Pinnacle's no-vig probability, in percent, with its standard error;
ROI is flat 1u at the Pinnacle close (so about -2.5 is break-even skill, the vig).

| Cut (bet the side named) | n | Edge vs close | ROI at Pinnacle | ROI at best price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Every favourite | 6,754 | +1.1 ±0.5 | -1.2 | +1.0 |
| Favourite hotter than the dog by 30%+ over their last 10 (bet fav) | 1,217 | +3.4 ±1.2 | +2.0 | +4.0 |
| ...ATP, favourite under 70% | 146 | +6.9 ±4.0 | +10.1 | +13.2 |
| ...ATP / WTA, favourite 70-85% | 185 / 234 | +4.8 ±3.0 / +6.3 ±2.7 | +3.9 / +5.3 | +6.0 / +7.5 |
| ...favourite 85%+ (both tours) | 479 | +2.2 ±1.3 | +0.0 | +1.4 |
| Dog hotter by 30%+ (bet dog) | 320 | +1.3 ±2.6 | +2.9 | +7.7 |

Read:
- Elo is not an edge against Pinnacle. Where Elo disagrees with the close the close is right, and the dogs Elo likes lose 11% of
  risk. Elo stays a modelling input (the set-1 curve's x, calibration), never a bet signal on its own.
- The market already prices the layoff, the surface switch, the lucky loser and the head-to-head; the structural residuals from
  section 8 were Elo missing information the market has. Betting the dog on any of those public reasons loses badly: the
  favourite-longshot bias is alive at slams (dogs with a story are over-bet).
- Form is the one thing that beats the close: a favourite whose last-10 record is 30%+ better than the dog's wins 3.4 points more
  than Pinnacle's price says (2.8 standard errors), positive in 6 of 8 years (2018 and 2022 the exceptions), flat-stake +2.0% at
  the close and +4.0% at the market's best price. Strongest at 60-85% favourites (+5 to +7 points), gone above 85% where the price
  has no room. Smaller and noisier the other way (hot dog, +1.3 ±2.6).
- Caveats before it is a system: slams only (the bundle), best-of-5 on the men's side, Pinnacle's close rather than an openable
  price, and form here is the last 10 matches at any level. It is a candidate for a paper tier C lean ("tennis hot favourite",
  0.25u: favourite 60-85% at the no-vig close, last-10 win rate 30%+ above the opponent's), not a live bet yet.

What would widen the test: the full tennis-data.co.uk workbooks (every ATP and WTA main-draw match since 2000 with Pinnacle, Bet365,
market max/avg: about 2,600 ATP and 2,400 WTA matches a year, five times the slam sample, and best-of-3). The site returns 403 to
GitHub's runner and the research container does not reach it; the yearly xlsx files download in a normal browser from
tennis-data.co.uk/alldata.php, and dropped into data/tennis/odds/ as atp_<year>.xlsx / wta_<year>.xlsx the script picks them up.

Findings:
- Fatigue from the previous match is the biggest new structural effect: a favourite off a long three-setter against a dog off a
  quick two-setter loses 6 points of match and 4.4 points of set 1 against its rating. Against the slam close (tennis_edge 9b-9e):
  nothing, +0.1 and +0.9 edge on the long/short cut, -1 to -4 on the sets cut, -4 on the comeback cut. Pinnacle prices fatigue; it
  is Elo that does not. Same lesson as the layoff and surface switch. And because the set-1 residual is proportional to the match
  residual (about 0.75 of it, like every other cut here), the live set-1 price built from the market match price already carries it.
  No set-1 adjustment.
- Slow starters do not exist as a persistent trait: a player's set-1 residual beyond the match residual correlates 0.07 season to
  season (3,305 player-seasons); the worst quintile this year (-9.9) is -1.0 next year. Tiebreak skill likewise: correlation 0.02
  across 4,913 player-seasons with 12+ tiebreaks; the best quartile (67%) wins 52% next year. Neither is a player input.
- Seeds carry information beyond Elo (dog seeded, fav not: -6.0), but seeding is rank, which the market has.
- Retirements: 2 to 3% of matches with a completed first set at every level (ATP slam hard courts 4.1%), flat across ages. The
  dog is the retiring player 51% of the time at a coin flip and 78% of the time against a 95% favourite: for a set-1 bet on a big
  favourite, a retirement after set 1 is almost always the dog's, so the set-1 market's settlement risk sits on the dog side.
- Straight sets by serve profile (the -1.5 set price): at a 60% favourite, 46% straight sets when the favourite is the bigger
  server, 38% when the dog is; at 75%, 58% vs 52%. The set-handicap fair price needs the hold profile, not just the match price.
- Set-1 margins (for set-1 handicaps): an ATP 80% favourite takes set 1 by 6-0 or 6-1 14% of the time and by four or more games
  29%; the dog takes it by four or more 4.5%. WTA runs 4 to 6 points higher on both.
- Altitude: clay tiebreak rate 17.6% vs 14.0% at sea level (ATP), set-1 games +0.2; favourite residuals -1.2 on altitude clay.
- Serve adjustment by surface (the SERVE_ADJ refit): dog bigger server -3.9 (hard), -3.8 (grass), -2.6 (clay); fav bigger +1.7 /
  +2.4 / +2.1; similar -1.3 / -1.3 / -0.6.

Board (11/4): the hot-favourite and serve-profile paper angles show on the board at slams only, where the price evidence is; tour
and Challenger picks are still logged and graded so the record builds by level (tennis_watch.json hot_record, serve_record).

Read:
- The games total is a serve story, not a "who wins" story. The match price sets P(3 sets) almost by itself (styles move it 1 to 3
  points); the two hold rates set everything else: two 85%+ holders play 1.7 more games, hit a tiebreak 16 points more often, and
  the first set reaches 10+ games 11 points more often. Weak returners add to it; strong returners take nothing off (returns are
  already in the hold rates).
- So the fair total-games line is baseline(tour, match price) + hold effect, and the set-1 games / tiebreak props follow the same
  input. These are the markets where a hold-rate model has something the match price does not say. Whether the books' totals
  already carry it is the open test: it needs totals lines, which the tennis logger does not record yet (Pinnacle lists them;
  Kalshi settles KXATPGTOTAL / KXWTAGTOTAL; Polymarket lists match and set-1 O/U lines on every event).
- For set-1 betting: the big-server dog's effect on the favourite is a set-1 story (fav straight sets -3.7 while 3-sets is flat),
  consistent with the live adjustment.
