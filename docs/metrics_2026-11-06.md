# Team metrics layered on the markets (11/6/2026)

Sean: combine the theories with team-level expected metrics (pace, xG, EPA, xFIP...). The test for each metric: does it add anything to
Pinnacle's own price (logistic on the result with the market's no-vig probability in the model)? A metric the market already prices
has zero weight; one it ignores shows up with a reliable weight and then gets tested as a bet. Everything pre-game: season-to-date from
EARLIER games only.

Sources (free): Bart Torvik game stats (CBB tempo and efficiency), MoneyPuck team game-by-game (NHL xG), nflverse play-by-play (NFL EPA).
Built: `sharp/metrics_cbb.py`, `sharp/metrics_nhl.py`, `sharp/metrics_nfl.py` -> `data/metrics/<sport>_pregame.json`.

## CBB tempo (25,694 games)
- Tempo adds nothing to Pinnacle + the curve on first-half totals, first-half spreads or team totals (z -0.6, -0.1, -0.2). The market
  prices pace.
- Inside the live first-half curve rule, the edge concentrates in fast games: gap 7+ in games 2+ possessions faster than the league,
  92-58 (+16.9%); mid-tempo 164-125 (+8.1%). Unders in fast games with a 4-7 point gap +2.2% vs slow -6.4%. One split, so it is a note on
  the rule, not a filter yet. Early season (both teams 5 or fewer games) is weakest (+2.0%).

## NHL expected goals (7,192 games)
- Season xG differential adds nothing to the closing moneyline (z +0.2); xG pace adds nothing to the closing total (z -1.1).
- Finishing luck (goal differential per 60 beyond xG differential) has a small negative weight (z -2.0): the market slightly overrates
  teams finishing above their xG. Fading the luckier team by 0.8+ goals per 60: 706-805, +3.0%, 5 of 7 seasons positive. Paper candidate.

## NFL EPA (1,710 games, 2020-25)
- EPA per play differential adds nothing to the closing spread (z -0.9); EPA, plays and points add nothing to the closing total.
- "EPA better than the scoreboard" teams ATS: 248-232 (-0.7%). Nothing.
